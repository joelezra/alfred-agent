## Deep Dive Research

### John Washam's Full Story

**Early life:** Delivered pizzas to save up for his first computer. "I was tired of being a broke kid." Bought his first PC in the 1990s.

**Military career:** Served in the US military as a translator stationed in South Korea. This was his first career before transitioning into software.

**The pivot:** Decided he wanted to work in software engineering at a major tech company. No computer science degree. Built a study plan from scratch.

**The preparation:**
- 8 months of intensive self-study
- 8-12 hours per day of studying and coding
- Focused on CS fundamentals: algorithms, data structures, systems
- Built the study list as a personal resource, tracking everything he needed to learn
- The first time he ever publicly talked about his story was a 2024 freeCodeCamp podcast

**The result:** Got hired as a Software Development Engineer at Amazon. Currently a senior SDE at Amazon/Zappos (Amazon subsidiary).

**The gift:** Instead of keeping his study plan private, he open-sourced it as "Coding Interview University" on GitHub. It became one of the most-starred repositories on all of GitHub.

**John's advice quote:** "You probably won't have to study as much as I did." He also recommends "The Talent Code" as reading for aspiring developers.

**Current status (2026):** Senior developer at Amazon (Zappos). Also runs a couple of companies. First-ever podcast interview was freeCodeCamp #134 (July 2024), episode titled "How to get a FAANG Dev Job in your 40s."

### Is It Still Relevant in 2026?

**Yes, but with context:**

The core curriculum (DSA, system design, OS, networking) is still the foundation of FAANG interviews. At Google, Amazon, Meta, and Apple, algorithmic puzzles are still ~80% of the coding interview content. They use LeetCode Hard as a filter and enforce proctoring.

**What's changed:**
- AI-aware coding rounds are now being piloted (Meta in late 2025: candidates can use GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet, Gemini 2.5 Pro during the problem — so pattern recognition and decomposition skill matters more than memorization)
- System design is 25-50% of interview for any role above Junior/L3
- Behavioral questions now often include AI literacy components

**How it compares to NeetCode:**
Coding Interview University is a comprehensive CS curriculum. NeetCode is a curated DSA problem list optimized specifically for interview prep.

| Feature | Coding Interview University | NeetCode 150 | LeetCode |
|---------|---------------------------|--------------|---------|
| CS theory | Comprehensive | None | None |
| Problem set | None | 150 curated | 2500+ problems |
| System design | Covered | Not included | Some premium |
| Free? | Fully free | Mostly free | Free tier |
| Time investment | 6-12 months | 6-8 weeks | Ongoing |
| Best for | Self-taught devs, theory gaps | Efficient interview prep | Practice volume |

**Community consensus for 2026:**
- Use Coding Interview University to understand the *why* behind DSA
- Use NeetCode 150 as your structured problem-solving curriculum
- Use LeetCode for volume practice and company-specific questions
- "Blind 75" is now largely superseded by NeetCode 150 (NeetCode 150 covers sliding window, backtracking, and greedy which Blind 75 misses)

### Study Path Recommendation for 2026

**If you have 3-4 months:**
1. Use NeetCode 150 as primary (covers 95% of algorithmic patterns seen in interviews)
2. Use Coding Interview University for any theory you don't understand (especially trees, graphs, sorting, Big-O)
3. Use LeetCode for company-specific problem tags in final 2-4 weeks
4. Add system design study (the repo has a section, but supplement with System Design Primer)

**If you have 6-12 months (self-taught, trying to break in):**
1. Work through Coding Interview University systematically — fork it, check boxes
2. Implement every data structure from scratch in your chosen language
3. Layer in NeetCode 150 problems as you complete each data structure section
4. Practice system design with mock interviews in months 5+
5. Do 200-300 LeetCode problems before starting interview process

**Key study habits (2026 best practices):**
- Study by pattern, not by problem (interviews test pattern recognition)
- Each problem 2-3 times over weeks using spaced repetition
- Do mock interviews — solo problem-solving doesn't simulate time pressure
- Anki flashcards for Big-O complexities and algorithm properties
