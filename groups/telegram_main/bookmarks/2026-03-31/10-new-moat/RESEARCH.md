## TL;DR
The "new moat" in the AI era is not AI capability itself — foundation models are now commodities available to everyone simultaneously. The real durable competitive advantage is proprietary organizational context (how work actually gets done, not how it's documented), accumulated domain data that competitors can't replicate, and deep workflow integration that creates switching costs. Multiple independent thinkers in Q1 2026 are converging on the same framework: when everyone has access to the same AI, what you feed that AI becomes everything.

## Key Claims / Insights
- Four of the five traditional moat pillars (switching costs, network effects, intangibles, efficient scale) now have "almost no predictive power" per Morningstar analysis
- AI foundation models are strategic commodities — when OpenAI/Anthropic/Google release new capabilities, all competitors gain access simultaneously
- "Less than 1% of enterprise data trained foundational models. AI has general intelligence. It lacks context."
- The new moat = proprietary data + organizational context + deep workflow integration + trust/governance
- Context is "demonstrated execution" — the workflows teams actually follow, the exceptions that trigger action, the judgment calls that repeat — visible only in behavior, not documentation
- "AI visibility moat" emerging: companies that appear as authoritative sources across AI search systems
- Greylock: Systems of Intelligence (apps combining multiple datasets + AI + proprietary data) remain the defensible model

### Why Traditional Moats Are Failing
Morningstar analysis: four of five classic moat pillars now have "almost no predictive power":
1. Switching costs — erode as AI makes migration easier
2. Network effects — can be replicated or bypassed by AI platforms
3. Intangible assets (patents, brands) — IP advantages erode as open-source models commoditize capabilities
4. Efficient scale — compute costs drop; cloud providers commoditize infrastructure

Only surviving traditional moat in AI era: **network effects at platform scale** (OpenAI's ecosystem, not individual app features)

### The Data Flywheel — The Only Sustainable Model
"The data network effect" or data flywheel:
1. Product usage generates unique proprietary data
2. That data trains/improves the AI model
3. Better AI → superior product
4. Superior product → more users
5. More users → more proprietary data (repeat)

Competitors cannot access this data. It compounds with time. It creates a moat that widens the longer you operate. This is why Flo Health (380M users, 10+ years of cycle data) is nearly impossible to displace by a new entrant — even with identical AI capabilities.

### Practical Implementation for Entrepreneurs (4 Steps)
From HBR's Murty/Kumar S framework:
1. Instrument work — map actual decision sequences and coordination patterns in your organization/product
2. Create a runtime context layer — connect AI systems to organizational patterns, not just formal documentation
3. Establish governance — protect sensitive information while making data AI-accessible
4. Monitor impact — link AI interactions to business metrics; prove the context layer is generating ROI

## Actionable Takeaways
1. Stop competing on which AI model you use — that's a commodity race you will lose
2. Identify what unique data your business generates that competitors cannot access — this is your moat
3. Instrument your actual workflows (not your documented ones) — map how decisions are really made
4. Build a "context layer" that connects your AI systems to your real organizational patterns
5. If pre-revenue: focus on accumulating domain-specific context before anything else — "you're not pre-revenue, you're pre-fundable" without it
6. Price on outcomes (per claim processed, per transaction) not per seat — this aligns incentives with your data flywheel
7. Pursue early adoption in regulated industries — compliance context is hardest to replicate and highest switching cost
8. Build AI visibility in your niche — be cited consistently by AI search systems (Perplexity, ChatGPT) as the authoritative source
9. Read the HBR piece by Murty/Kumar S (Feb 2026) — most actionable 4-step framework for building context moats
10. The winning formula: pick a vertical, go deep, accumulate context over time — horizontal AI plays lose to specialized context holders
