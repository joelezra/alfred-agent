## Deep Dive
This is a classic comment-bait lead magnet post — the real content is gated behind a DM. The tweet itself is a framework outline, not the full system. The engagement pattern (105 replies, nearly all just "PIPELINE") confirms this is a distribution tactic as much as content.

The underlying thesis is well-supported by 2026 LinkedIn data: LinkedIn's algorithm now weights "Depth Score" (time on content, saves, substantive comments) over vanity likes. InMail delivers 10-25% response rates vs. cold email's 1-5%. LinkedIn has also launched a Sales Navigator AI Assistant and conversational search in early 2026, making prospect identification faster.

The three-pillar framework Yann describes is sound:
1. ICP definition before any content creation (common mistake: skipping this step)
2. Content that magnetically attracts ICP rather than just broadcasting
3. Warm outreach sequences that feel earned (voice notes are a strong differentiator — they're unusual enough to stand out)

## Actionable Takeaways
- Build ICP definition before any LinkedIn content — specificity is the entire game
- Use content to warm leads; DM only after someone has engaged with your posts
- Voice notes in DMs = significant differentiator in 2026 (few people do it)
- Comment-bait lead magnets are a legitimate distribution tactic — can replicate this format
- If building B2B GTM: LinkedIn is worth testing as a primary channel, not just supplementary
- The sequence: post content → profile views → warm DM → voice note follow-up → booked call
