# Jack Dorsey / Block — "From Hierarchy to Intelligence" Essay

**Source:** https://block.xyz/inside/from-hierarchy-to-intelligence
**Author:** Jack Dorsey (Block CEO) + Roelof Botha (Sequoia Capital partner, Block's lead independent director)
**Bookmarked:** 2026-03-31
**Category:** AI / Claude Code Technical
**Type:** Insight (strategic framing with direct implications for AI-native org design)
**Engagement:** CoinDesk, FinanceFeeds, Decrypt, Substack analyses; 17% Block share price jump on announcement

## TL;DR

Published March 31, 2026, Dorsey and Botha argue that corporate hierarchy has always existed solely to route information — and AI can now do that function continuously and at scale, making middle management structurally redundant. Block eliminated ~4,000 positions (40–50% headcount) and proposes replacing the management layer with two AI "world models" (company + customer) feeding an intelligence layer that dynamically assembles financial products. Only three roles remain: Individual Contributors, Directly Responsible Individuals (DRIs), and Player-Coaches.

## Key Claims

- Corporate hierarchy is a 2,000-year-old solution to one problem: routing information in organizations too large for one person to oversee
- AI can perform management's information-relay function continuously and at scale
- Only 3 roles needed: ICs (deep specialists), DRIs (cross-functional problem owners), Player-Coaches (hands-on builders + mentors)
- Two world models replace managers: Company World Model (internal operations) + Customer World Model (transaction behavior)
- Block's economic graph: observing both sides of millions of transactions daily (buyers via Cash App, sellers via Square)
- Intelligence layer dynamically assembles financial products from existing capabilities
- Block eliminated ~4,000 positions in late February 2026 (40–50% headcount)
- $450–500M one-time restructuring charge
- Block share price jumped ~17% on announcement
- "This was not a cost reduction but a permanent restructuring"

## Deep Dive

### The Historical Argument

The essay opens with the Roman Army: the smallest unit was the contubernium (8 soldiers, led by a decanus), ten contubernia made a century (80 men, led by a centurion). This hierarchy solved a coordination problem — no Roman general could directly manage thousands of individuals. The nested hierarchy was the information routing system.

Dorsey's argument: corporate org charts are the same invention. Managers aggregate context from reports below, transmit directives from leadership above, and maintain alignment across teams. They are information relay nodes. The essay argues this relay function — not the human judgment function — is what AI replaces.

This is a sharper claim than "AI makes everyone more productive." It's "the coordinator role is structurally eliminated, not augmented."

### The Three Role Types (in detail)

**Individual Contributors (ICs)**
Deep specialists operating at specific system layers. Under the new model, they receive context directly from the company's world model rather than waiting for managerial interpretation. Freed from status meetings and alignment sessions, ICs work at higher velocity with better context than the old relay system provided.

**Directly Responsible Individuals (DRIs)**
The DRI concept comes from Steve Jobs-era Apple but Dorsey sharpens it: a DRI "owns the problem of merchant churn in a specific segment for 90 days, with full authority to pull resources" from multiple teams. Time-boxed, outcome-defined, full authority. The role rotates as problems are solved. No permanent management hierarchy — temporary ownership of specific outcomes.

**Player-Coaches**
The only management-adjacent role retained. Player-Coaches combine active technical work with mentoring. Unlike traditional managers whose job is "status meetings, alignment sessions, and priority negotiations," Player-Coaches contribute code or product work while developing other ICs. This preserves the human growth function of management while eliminating the relay function.

### The Two World Models

**Company World Model**
A continuously updated machine-readable operational picture incorporating: all decisions made, all code written, all designs created, all problems encountered, all performance data. Every IC and DRI has access to the same operational picture the CEO has. No information asymmetry from management layers.

**Customer World Model**
A per-customer understanding built from transaction data. Block's structural advantage: it observes both sides of millions of transactions every day — the buyer through Cash App and the seller through Square. The essay calls this the "economic graph."

The world models are not BI dashboards. The essay describes them as the AI's operating context — the environment in which the intelligence layer reasons and acts.

### The Intelligence Layer and Dynamic Product Assembly

The intelligence layer sits above both world models and does the work managers used to do: identifying needs, composing solutions, generating backlog.

Essay example: "When the system identifies a need — the essay's example is a merchant facing a seasonal cash flow gap — it assembles a solution from existing capabilities." When it can't assemble from existing capabilities, the missing capability defines what gets built next. Product roadmap is replaced by system-generated backlog.

This is the most radical claim in the essay. It implies product management (in its traditional form) is also structurally eliminated — the system surfaces what needs to be built.

### The Economic Graph

Block's data moat is the economic graph: deep understanding of merchant and customer financial behavior that compounds continuously. Because Block sees both sides of transactions (buyer + seller) at massive scale (millions of merchants, tens of millions of Cash App consumers), its customer world model is richer than any competitor that sees only one side.

This directly connects to Michael Bloch's moat framework referenced in the March 30 bookmarks: "compounding data" as a defensible moat. Block's intelligence layer is only valuable if the economic graph is proprietary. The organizational transformation is downstream of the data advantage.

### Block's Specific Situation

- Block's headcount before cuts: approximately 8,000–10,000 employees
- Eliminated: ~4,000 positions (including engineering, product, operations, and management layers)
- Timing: late February 2026, weeks before essay publication
- Framing from Dorsey: "strategic acceleration," not distress
- Financial impact: $450–500M one-time charge; 17% share price increase

### The Criticism

**Wharton professor Ethan Mollick:** Given how new effective AI tools are, it is difficult to imagine a firm-wide efficiency gain large enough to justify such sweeping cuts.

**Current and former Block employees (The Guardian):** Approximately 95% of AI-generated code changes still require human modification. AI tools cannot yet lead in regulated environments (Block operates in financial services — heavily regulated).

**The implementation gap:** The essay describes a target state. Block has not (as of the essay) fully implemented the two world models or the intelligence layer. The headcount reduction preceded the infrastructure that would replace it.

**AI authorship question:** Wired and others noted the essay itself may be substantially AI-generated, raising questions about whether Dorsey's team practices what is preached.

### Sequoia's Role

Co-author Roelof Botha (Sequoia partner, Block board) makes this essay more than a CEO think piece. Sequoia simultaneously published it, suggesting this is a framework Sequoia intends to promote to its portfolio companies. Sequoia's involvement implies financial and organizational consulting support for companies attempting this restructuring.

## Actionable Takeaways

1. **The DRI model is immediately applicable at startup scale** — assign time-boxed problem ownership with full resource authority rather than permanent management hierarchies. For a small AI-native operation, this is already the natural structure.
2. **Build a "company world model" as a file system, not a dashboard** — a machine-readable operational state (decisions, code, designs, problems) is what CLAUDE.md and the Knowledge Ledger architecture in `02-claude-memory-architecture` are building toward.
3. **The "economic graph" concept applies at micro scale** — if you observe both sides of a transaction (merchant + buyer data), you have a structural data advantage. Any product that captures both sides is building this moat.
4. **Player-Coach > Manager for AI-native teams** — the role that survives is the one that combines hands-on work with mentoring. Pure coordinators are structurally at risk.
5. **Watch for Sequoia portfolio implementations** — Sequoia is actively promoting this framework. The companies that implement it first will either prove or disprove the thesis at scale. Observe the outcomes.
