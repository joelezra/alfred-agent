## TL;DR

Jack Dorsey and Sequoia's Roelof Botha published an essay titled "From Hierarchy to Intelligence" on March 31, 2026, following Block's elimination of ~4,000 positions (roughly 40-50% of headcount). The thesis: corporate hierarchy exists solely to route information through large organizations — something AI can now do continuously and at scale. The essay proposes replacing management layers with two AI "world models" and three human roles only. Rohan Paul synthesized this clearly with high-quality analysis. This is one of the most significant corporate AI strategy documents of 2026.

---

## Key Claims

- Block cut ~4,000 jobs in late February 2026 — framed explicitly as strategic acceleration, not distress. Gross profit was rising, guidance was raised, stock surged 20%+.
- The essay "From Hierarchy to Intelligence" was co-authored by Dorsey and Roelof Botha (Sequoia/Block's lead independent director), hosted at block.xyz/inside/from-hierarchy-to-intelligence.
- Dorsey told Wired the restructuring was triggered by a capability shift observed in December 2025 in Anthropic's Opus 4.6 and OpenAI's Codex 5.3, which could now operate effectively in large codebases.
- Block has imposed a hiring freeze through at least end of 2026, backfilling departures only selectively — except for AI specialists.
- Block's "economic graph" gives them access to transaction data from both sides: Cash App buyers and Square sellers, plus merchant operational data. This compounds daily.
- Key quote from Dorsey/Botha: "At Block, we're questioning the underlying assumption: that organizations have to be hierarchically organized with humans as the coordination mechanism. Instead, we intend to replace what the hierarchy does… a company built as an intelligence (or mini-AGI)."

## Deep Dive

### The Historical Framework (from the essay itself)

The essay traces 2,000 years of organizational evolution:
- Roman Army: Span of control principle (3-8 per leader) → nested hierarchy
- Prussian Military (1806): General Staff as dedicated information processors after defeat at Jena
- American Railroads (1840s-1850s): Daniel McCallum created the first org chart to manage 500-mile networks
- Frederick Taylor: Measurement-based management and specialization
- Post-WWII: McKinsey's matrix organization balancing function and division
- Recent experiments: Spotify squads, Zappos Holacracy, Valve flat structure — all reverted to hierarchy at scale

The key insight: every experiment failed because no technology could replace hierarchy's information-routing function. AI changes this for the first time.

### The Proposed Architecture

*Company World Model:* Remote-first work creates machine-readable artifacts (decisions, code, designs, problems). AI builds a continuously updated picture of operations, replacing the context managers traditionally carried.

*Customer World Model:* Transaction data from both sides of the market → per-customer/merchant/market financial reality model. Compounds every second.

*Intelligence Layer:* Composes capabilities dynamically. Example given: A restaurant facing seasonal cash tightness receives a short-term loan offer before asking, because the system recognized the pattern and assembled the solution. No product manager required.

*Interfaces:* Square, Cash App, Afterpay, TIDAL, bitkey, proto — important but not where value is created.

### Three Human Roles Only

1. Individual Contributors: Deep specialists building capabilities, models, intelligence layer, interfaces
2. Directly Responsible Individuals (DRIs): Own specific cross-cutting problems for defined periods (e.g., merchant churn in a specific segment for 90 days), with authority to pull resources across teams
3. Player-Coaches: Combine building with people development — no status meetings, world model handles alignment

No permanent middle management layer. System coordinates; everyone operates at the edge.

### Criticism and Pushback

- Current and former Block employees told The Guardian that roughly 95% of AI-generated code changes still require human modification and AI cannot yet lead in regulated areas like banking and money transfers
- Wharton's Ethan Mollick: given how new effective AI tools are, it is difficult to imagine firm-wide efficiency gains large enough to justify such sweeping cuts
- Reply in thread (@kyzookyzoo): "Middle management wasn't just routing information — it was absorbing blame and translating executive fantasy into plausible roadmaps. AI can coordinate work, but who takes the fall when the model optimizes for metrics that destroy morale?"
- Reply (@Criticality47): "AI can kill status relay. It does not kill the moment two teams both have clean dashboards and still want opposite things."
- DSTL comment in thread: "The mispricing isn't in the RIF itself. It's that Block's gross profit per employee should start diverging from Stripe and PayPal within 4-6 quarters if the production function really broke." (this is the most analytically interesting reply — watch Block gross profit / headcount vs. Stripe/PayPal)
- Broader concern: some analysts call this "narrative substitution" — reframing operational challenges as AI transformation to appease investors

### What Rohan Paul Added

Rohan Paul's synthesis is strong. He frames the core mechanism clearly: managers are "narrow-band routers of context" — they collect fragments, compress them into summaries, push decisions back through layers. Each compression step introduces latency and distortion. The CEO gets a lossy, high-latency, low-bandwidth view of reality. AI world models are potentially zero-latency, high-bandwidth, lossless. The infrastructure that makes this possible is what the thread commenter @akoratana calls a "context graph."

Key Paul insight: "Money is behavior with fewer illusions attached. If you can see how customers and merchants actually spend, borrow, save, and repay, you are no longer guessing from survey answers or product roadmaps."

## Actionable Takeaways

1. Read the actual Block essay — it's the most concrete AI-org-design document from a major tech company so far. block.xyz/inside/from-hierarchy-to-intelligence
2. Track Block's gross profit per employee vs. Stripe and PayPal quarterly — this is the real test of whether the AI production function thesis holds (per DSTL comment)
3. The "DRI on 90-day cycles" model is immediately replicable — it's cleaner than standard PM ownership and forces explicit handoffs
4. Block's competitive moat claim rests on the economic graph (both sides of Cash App/Square transactions). If you're building in fintech, this is the comparison point: do you have behavioral data from both sides of a transaction?
5. The hiring freeze + AI specialist exception is a structural signal: watch for this pattern spreading to other tech companies in H2 2026
6. "Intelligence as a product not a layer" is the strategic frame — if your company's understanding of customers doesn't compound daily, AI is just a cost optimization story
