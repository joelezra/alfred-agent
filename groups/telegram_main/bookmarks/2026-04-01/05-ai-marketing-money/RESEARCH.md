### The "Larry Loop" — AI Marketing Agent on OpenClaw

The most specific and actionable piece of content from this creator context is the Larry Loop, documented in a podcast episode:

**What Larry does:**
1. Creates TikTok slideshow content autonomously (not just drafts — actually publishes)
2. Reads performance analytics after publishing
3. Iterates on hooks and CTAs based on performance data
4. Feeds that data back into the content creation loop

**The result:** Oliver Henry (the founder who built Larry) generates "hundreds of dollars in monthly recurring revenue from mobile apps he barely touches" — the agent runs the marketing while he does other work.

**The infrastructure:** Larry was built on OpenClaw as a skill, then packaged as a free downloadable skill on Larry Brain, allowing anyone to replicate it. This is a direct example of skills becoming the new form of SaaS distribution.

**Connection to @ErnestoSOFTWARE:** Ernesto's "Eddie" agent (bookmark 3) follows the exact same architecture — an AI marketing agent named as a persona, running on an agent platform, handling content creation and analytics. Multiple founders converging on this pattern independently.

### The Promoter Blueprint (Jonathan Courtney)

From the podcast episode, Jonathan outlined a four-phase revenue system:

1. **Traffic generation** — AI creates content at scale across platforms
2. **Holding pattern** — nurture the audience captured from that content (email, community)
3. **Selling event** — a focused event (webinar, launch, limited offer) that converts
4. **Conversion + feedback loop** — what worked feeds back into phase 1

The $450K webinar campaign was built using this blueprint with Claude for research and marketing assets, and Claude Code for rapid shipping of landing pages and automation. This is not a theoretical framework — it was a single campaign result.

Key observation: "A CEO's primary job is promoting the business — building is secondary to getting people in the door." The podcast repeatedly cautions against "procrastination machines" — founders who over-optimize systems with zero customers.

### The 30-Step SaaS Playbook (Greg Isenberg)

Greg's standalone playbook for 2026 B2C/SaaS:

1. Start in a specific sub-niche, not a broad market
2. Begin as a service business — manually perform the workflow before automating
3. Map the entire workflow end to end before writing code
4. Create scroll-stopping content in the niche to build audience
5. Double down on organic while running paid ads on proven winners
6. Capture emails from day one
7. Hire from the niche (they understand the customers)
8. Reinvest profits, become the default layer in the market

The "start as a service business" step is particularly important and often ignored by builders — doing the work manually teaches you what to automate.

## Actionable Takeaways

1. Build a "Larry Loop" for any app or product: agent creates content → reads analytics → iterates → repeat. This is replicable on OpenClaw with a skill template.
2. Use the Promoter Blueprint sequence before any product launch: traffic → nurture → event → convert. The webinar pattern ($450K from Claude-assisted campaign) is the highest-leverage selling event.
3. "Start as a service business" — before building an AI marketing product, manually run the workflow for 3-5 clients to understand what to automate and where the real pain is
4. The sub-niche play: the smaller and more specific the audience, the less competition and the easier the content targeting. 5,000 engaged users in a tight niche > 50,000 general subscribers.
5. Larry Brain (skill sharing platform) is worth monitoring — if skills become the distribution mechanism for AI capabilities, publishing skills there is an early distribution channel
6. Greg's free AI workshop (startup-ideas-pod.link/build-with-ai-2026) is potentially worth attending for the 30-step playbook in full detail
