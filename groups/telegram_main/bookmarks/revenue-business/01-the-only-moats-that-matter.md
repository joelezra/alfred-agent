## The Core Thesis

> "Defensibility has always come from two things: things that are hard to do and things that are hard to get. AI is making the first one worthless."

**AI compresses the time it takes to do things. It does not compress the time it takes for things to happen.**

This single distinction is now the most important filter for evaluating whether a business is defensible.

---

## The 5 Moats That Survive AI

### 1. Compounding Proprietary Data
Not all data qualifies. A static dataset that was merely expensive to collect will eventually be synthesized or worked around. That's "hard to do" — increasingly easy with AI.

**The real moat:** *Living data* — proprietary information continuously generated through operations that are themselves defensible.

**Example:** Orchard AI mounts cameras to farm equipment, tracking billions of fruit across millions of trees across multiple growing seasons. The data changes every day. Every pass through an orchard makes the dataset richer and the models smarter. You can't replicate that by training on public data — you'd have to drive the same cameras through the same orchards for years.

### 2. Network Effects
Every user that joins makes the product more valuable for every other user.

**Example:** DoorDash — every driver makes delivery faster, every restaurant gives more choice, every customer improves economics. Clone the app overnight. The drivers, restaurants, customers, and delivery density in ten thousand cities don't come with it.

**New wrinkle in AI era:** The cold start problem may get *harder* — AI makes it trivial to build competitors, so a hundred well-built alternatives fight to bootstrap the same network. Whoever already has the liquidity compounds. Everyone else fights over scraps.

### 3. Regulatory Permission
Governments move at the speed of politics, not technology. And the surface area of regulation is **expanding** as AI capability increases — not shrinking.

**Example:** Anduril needs procurement clearances and classified contracts. A bank charter takes years. FDA approval takes years. No amount of AI compresses these timelines.

### 4. Capital at Scale
The most underweighted moat. The endgame is physical.

- Chip fabs: $20B
- Nuclear plants: $10B
- Satellite constellations: $Bs more

Capital access isn't just money — it's institutional trust, track record, and relationships that take decades to build.

**Example:** Elon raising $75B and IPOing SpaceX. The bottleneck is shifting from software to atoms.

### 5. Physical Infrastructure
Factories, power plants, battery networks, data centres.

**Example:** Base Power deploys thousands of battery units across Texas homes while building its own manufacturing facility. Every unit installed is a physical asset generating revenue. Soon you can design the system in a week with AI. You cannot manufacture, install, and interconnect thousands of units in a week. **Physics sets a floor on timelines that intelligence can't break.**

## What Didn't Make the List

| "Moat" | Why It's Dying |
|--------|----------------|
| Workflow embeddedness | Switching cost is engineering time in disguise — AI makes migrations trivial |
| Ecosystem lock-in | AI can rebuild integrations as fast as you can describe them |
| Software scale | Spreading engineering costs across users stops mattering when engineering costs → 0 |

These were moats against the *scarcity of intelligence*. That scarcity is ending.

---

## The Meta-Moat

> "Time that can't be parallelised is the meta-moat underneath all five."

Network density takes years of human adoption. Regulatory approval takes years of political process. Infrastructure takes years to build. Data takes years to compound. Capital relationships take decades to earn.

**The companies that already hold these positions aren't just defensible — they're pulling further ahead every day, because the head start is the moat.**

## Open Questions (from Bloch)

- Does **trust** become its own moat? When AI does more of the work, someone has to be accountable when things go wrong. The institution bearing liability may become more valuable, not less.
- Is **human attention** a moat? When content creation cost → 0, brands that already hold attention may have the most compounding advantage.

Both feel real. Neither cleanly fits the five categories above.

## What This Means for Your SaaS Ideas

The research in `/workspace/group/research/` identified several SaaS opportunities. Run each one through this filter:

**Bad moat (AI will erode):** Better UX, faster onboarding, cheaper pricing, more features, better integrations
**Good moat (survives AI):** Network effects between users, data that compounds with use, regulatory positioning, distribution you own

The feedback/roadmap tool we researched scores okay on distribution (widget embeds into customer products) but needs a network effect layer to be truly defensible. Think: how does having more users make it more valuable for all users?

## Key Soundbite

> "Hard to do, or hard to get? If your moat is bottlenecked by intelligence, you're on borrowed time. If it's bottlenecked by years, by human behaviour, by physics, by political will, by capital — you're probably building something that lasts."
