# How the 3-month fastest growers get customers

Studied 2026-11-02. These are the 12 TrustMRR businesses that grew steadily from
Jul to Oct 2026. One file per business in this folder; this page has the patterns.
Caveats: the Meta Ad Library and Reddit search were blocked, so ad spend is
inferred from tracking code on each site, never seen directly.

## Patterns

**1. Nobody has an audience.** Founders have 0–3k X followers. Brand TikTok,
YouTube and Instagram accounts are tiny everywhere (tens to ~1k followers). Building
in public and personal brand drove none of this growth.

**2. Four ways they get customers anyway:**
- **Intent search, often in a small market or language**: STRK, TollTracker,
  PokéItem (App Store search); NETTIFY (Norwegian), Aisle (programmatic SEO). They
  go after people already searching, in places big players ignore.
- **Paid ads into a tuned funnel**: White Glove, flint, DirectRoles. Pixels for
  every ad network, many landing-page and price variants, weekly pricing, exit
  coupons. They grow by buying traffic and optimizing conversion.
- **Other people's audiences do the selling**: ZebraSnap (photographers send
  their own followers), Telestars (agencies and affiliates), Autoclip (Whop
  clippers, watermark), PokéItem (users earn XP for referrals and clips), Get
  Funneled (agencies earn commission).
- **Founder sales calls**: Get Funneled, a high-ticket service (~$850/mo) with
  only ~22 clients.

**3. Timing beats volume.** TollTracker shipped 3 days after a new law took
effect. STRK took the name of a competitor that had disappeared. PokéItem's
revenue doubled the month its prize contest launched. TollTracker's country
launches and Autoclip's daily blog posts line up with their growth curves.

**4. A wave of SEO pages in October**: Aisle, Autoclip, NETTIFY, ZebraSnap, Telestars,
TollTracker all added hundreds to thousands of generated pages. For most of them
this came *after* the growth, a second channel stacked on a working first one, so
don't credit SEO for the curve.

**5. Revenue isn't always subscriptions.** Aisle (MRR $2.5k vs $24.8k revenue),
Telestars (~7% subscriptions; the rest is a cut of creators' sales) and ZebraSnap
(possibly gross photo sales) take a cut of money flowing through them. That
lifts headline revenue well above what they actually earn.

**6. Many are for sale.** flint, White Glove ($2.5M ask), Get Funneled, NETTIFY.
NETTIFY's founder wrote that "distribution in the small Norwegian market was the
bottleneck". Sale listings are a good source of founder-written insight.

## Playbook to copy

1. Pick a niche where people already search with intent (App Store or Google)
   and the big players aren't paying attention: a local language, a new rule, a dead competitor.
2. Build distribution into the product: referral credit, rewards for posting,
   a watermark on free output, a commission for partners who already have the audience.
3. If you buy ads, treat the funnel as the product: price tests, weekly plans,
   exit offers, a pixel for every network.
4. Add programmatic SEO pages *after* one channel already works.
5. Skip personal brand-building as the main plan; none of these needed it.

## Red flags noticed

- DirectRoles: hardcoded testimonials with stock avatars, fake "found it on Reddit"
  quotes, and fake progress screens. Copy the funnel mechanics, not the deception.
